# Known issues and limits

Read this before you deploy urMQTT. It lists what is known to be missing, weak or unproven, what each item means
for you, what to do about it, and where it is tracked. It is a summary: the sources are
[AUDIT.md](AUDIT.md) sections 5 and 6 (weaknesses and accepted risks), [ROADMAP.md](ROADMAP.md) (planned work),
the "Limits, stated plainly" paragraphs of [CHANGELOG.md](../CHANGELOG.md) for v0.4.0 to v0.6.0, and
[EDITIONS.md](EDITIONS.md). `scripts/check-known-issues.py` (run in CI) fails if an AUDIT id or ROADMAP item cited
here no longer exists, or if an AUDIT weakness cited here has been marked fixed.

Status as of v0.6.0 (2026-11-09). **The project is alpha.** The three items of the definition of "alpha" (a real
release signing key, the entropy decision and the MTU fix; [ROADMAP.md](ROADMAP.md), "Community readiness") are in
v0.4.2, the first release signed with the release key, and in v0.5.0 and v0.6.0; the owner changed the status word on 2026-11-07.
Alpha means: fit for trials and pilots whose operator reads this document, not for unattended production.

## 2. Protocol and features

| Issue | What it means for you | Workaround | Tracking |
|---|---|---|---|
| **MQTT 5.0 without enhanced authentication and without topic aliases from the broker.** A CONNECT with an Authentication Method gets CONNACK 0x8C and an AUTH packet after a CONNECT DISCONNECT 0x82; the broker never sends a topic alias (a client's aliases to it work). The reason codes 0x96 (message rate too high), 0x89 (server busy), 0x8A (banned) and 0x9F (connection rate exceeded) are never sent: the rate limit refuses a PUBLISH with 0x97 and keeps the connection, admission and lock-outs act before there is a CONNECT to answer. MQTT 3.1 (protocol level 3) is still refused. | A client that needs a SASL-style exchange cannot use one; a client that waits for one of those four codes sees a 0x97 acknowledgement or a refused TCP connection instead. | A client certificate or a plaintext/WebSocket account for identity. | ADR-275; ROADMAP NEXT-3 |
| **Wills do not survive a broker restart and are not published at `reboot`.** A Will, armed or waiting for its Will Delay Interval, lives in RAM. The console's `reboot` tells 5.0 clients DISCONNECT 0x8B and publishes no Will; a crash or power cut loses the Wills too. | A subscriber that watches Wills to detect dead devices learns nothing from a broker restart; a delayed Will pending at the restart never goes out. | Treat a broker restart (`$SYS/broker/uptime`, the 0x8B a 5.0 client receives) as "every device must reconnect"; the persistent sessions are found again after it. | ADR-275 |
| **No order between connections that do not wait for each other.** The SUBACK is sent after the subscription is in effect, so a message published after the subscriber received its SUBACK is delivered. A client that publishes right after another connection sent its SUBSCRIBE, without waiting for that SUBACK, can be routed before the SUBSCRIBE is read on a different core, and the message misses the new subscription. For a shared subscription the SUBACK waits for the core that owns the group, so the same rule holds. | Test harnesses that subscribe on one connection and publish on another at once may miss the first messages (the Paho suite does this in two tests, docs/MQTT5-CONFORMANCE.md). MQTT promises no order between connections. | Publish after the SUBACK reached the subscriber. | ADR-275 |
| **Shared subscriptions are grouped per role class; `$SYS` shares see one core.** A group is (role class, group name, filter): a device and a service subscribing to `$share/g/t/#` are in two groups and each group gets every message. An operator's share of `$SYS/...` gets only the `$SYS` messages of the data core that owns the group (each core publishes its own). The limits `limits.max_shared_groups` and `limits.max_shared_group_members` count per data core and per group. | Consumers of one group must have the same role class. A `$SYS` statistics consumer gets one core's figures through a share. | Subscribe to `$SYS/broker/#` without `$share/`. | ADR-275 |
| **MQTT over WebSocket: TLS only, no compression, password sessions are clean only.** The `[websocket]` listener serves `wss://` (never `ws://`), agrees on no extension (no permessage-deflate), and its `auth = "password"` sessions cannot ask for a stored session. | A browser client's subscriptions do not survive a reconnect; it must subscribe again. Accounts change by building a new image, as for plaintext. | Subscribe again on every connect (MQTT.js does with `clean: true`); a client that needs queued messages uses `auth = "mtls"` with a certificate. | ADR-272; ROADMAP NOW-15 |
| **No bridging, no webhooks; the only outbound connection is log shipping over TLS** (`observability.log_tls`, off unless configured; ADR-315 to ADR-319). The collector is an IPv4 literal, no client certificate is offered, delivery is at most once (up to 64 KiB written into a connection that then breaks may be lost). | No forwarding to another broker, no webhooks; statsd stays plain UDP. | A service identity (`acl.service_ids`) that subscribes and forwards from outside. | ADR-319; ROADMAP NEXT-7 (done), NEXT-8 |
| **Offline persistent sessions expire, also 3.1.1 ones (behaviour change).** A session offline for longer than its Session Expiry Interval is deleted with its subscriptions and queue; a 3.1.1 persistent session (`clean_session = 0`) gets `limits.default_session_expiry_s`, 30 days by default; a session that ran out while the broker was down is dropped at the next boot. Expired retained messages are cleared by a sweep (at most 64 per 100 ms tick), when they are read and at the next boot. | A device offline for more than 30 days comes back to an empty session (session present 0) and must subscribe again; its queued messages are gone. | Raise `limits.default_session_expiry_s` (and `limits.max_session_expiry_s`; 4 294 967 295 never expires) for fleets that sleep longer; a 5.0 client asks for what it needs. | ADR-274, ADR-275 |
| **The per-address limit on established TLS connections is 1024 by default (behaviour change in v0.6.0).** `limits.max_connections_per_ip` counts the MQTT and WebSocket ports together; over it a SYN gets no answer. An IPv6 /64 counts as one address. The table holds 32 768 addresses (beyond that a new address is let through uncounted and counted); there is no per-identity limit, no /24 grouping and no exemption list. | Devices behind one NAT share an address: a site with more than 1024 devices behind one address is refused beyond 1024. | Set `limits.max_connections_per_ip` higher (or 0) before the update; issue certificates only to devices you control and revoke a misbehaving one. | ADR-280; ROADMAP NOW-14 |
| **A plaintext password check is PBKDF2 at 100 000 iterations by default, at most 200 000, below OWASP's 600 000, and not memory-hard (no Argon2).** The check runs in one go on a data core for every login. | A stolen image's hashes of a short or human-chosen password can be guessed offline on GPUs; a login holds its core for about 25 ms (HVF) to an estimated 50 ms (VPS vCPU), and new password logins are admitted at about 10 to 20 a second per data core. | Long random passwords (the builder's generator; `urmqtt-config hash-password` for your own); treat the image as a secret; fewer iterations (`--iterations`) if a fleet must reconnect faster. | ADR-267, ROADMAP D-16 |
| **A control message's sender learns nothing about its result.** | A PUBACK means "received", not "applied". | Read the broker log or `urmqtt.control.applied` / `rejected.*`. | AUDIT H1-W1 |
| **Metrics are plain HTTP behind an allow-list, statsd and syslog are plain UDP.** The Prometheus endpoint (`[metrics]`, TCP 9100, off by default) has no TLS, no authentication and no histograms; the allow-list is the only control. Syslog can go over TLS (`observability.log_tls`); statsd cannot. | Anyone on an allowed network can read every counter; UDP to a collector can be read or forged on the path. | Allow only your Prometheus's address; keep the collectors on a private network. | ADR-290, ADR-294; ROADMAP NEXT-9 |
| **Run-time password accounts are basic.** `set-account` and `remove-account` (ADR-285): three classes, no per-account ACL, no listing over the network, no bulk import, no expiry, no per-account rate limit; PBKDF2 as above. The x86_64 smoke group `accounts` has not run (it needs KVM). | An account's rights are its class narrowed by the listener's patterns. | Use the classes; keep the account list in your own records. | ADR-289; ROADMAP NOW-15 |

## 3. Network

| Issue | What it means for you | Workaround | Tracking |
|---|---|---|---|
| **IPv6 is basic** (since NEXT-1): one global address, no fragment reassembly, no ICMPv6 errors or path MTU discovery, no DHCPv6; SNTP, syslog, statsd and log shipping are IPv4 only; `plaintext.ip_identities` holds IPv4 networks only. | A second advertised prefix is ignored; a path with an MTU below the router's loses large segments; an IPv6 client of a plaintext listener with `auth = "ip"` is refused. | The router's MTU option or `network.mtu`; IPv4 collectors; `auth = "password"` for IPv6 clients. | ADR-333 |
| **The MTU: up to v0.4.1 assumed to be 1500 bytes.** Fixed in v0.4.2 (DHCP option 26 and `network.mtu`, ADR-264), tested under QEMU only (smoke group `net_mtu`); nothing has run on Google Cloud. | On a network with a smaller MTU (Google Cloud's default network: 1460) v0.4.1 and older lose large segments without an error. | v0.4.2; with a static address, set `network.mtu` (configuration format 7, read by v0.4.2 and newer only). | ROADMAP NOW-19 |
| **No DNS.** | SNTP servers and collectors are IPv4 literals. | Give addresses, not names. | ROADMAP NEXT-10 |
| **Many VPS providers run no DHCP for a custom ISO.** | With `network.mode = "dhcp"` the broker gets no address. | `"static"` with the provider's values, or `"auto"` (DHCP first, then static). | [DEPLOY.md](DEPLOY.md), README "Configuring the network of a VPS" |

## 4. Platforms and devices

| Issue | What it means for you | Workaround | Tracking |
|---|---|---|---|
| **virtio over PCI only.** No drivers for AWS ENA or NVMe, Google gVNIC, Azure MANA/NetVSC, VMware VMXNET3/PVSCSI, physical NICs or disk controllers. | A production image without a virtio-net NIC stops with a message that names the NIC and says what to attach; a disk that is not virtio is not used (no data partition). | A machine type that offers virtio-net and virtio-blk or virtio-scsi (KVM, Proxmox, netcup, Contabo, Hetzner, Google Cloud e2/n2, ...). The drivers are the commercial edition's scope. | [EDITIONS.md](EDITIONS.md); ROADMAP LATER-2, LATER-3, LATER-4 |
| **No Firecracker** (virtio over MMIO, no firmware). | Not supported: the kernel's virtio transport is PCI and it is started by UEFI or BIOS firmware. | A hypervisor with UEFI or BIOS and virtio-pci. | [EDITIONS.md](EDITIONS.md) section 2 |
| **Legacy-only (pre-1.0) virtio devices are refused**, and disks with 4096-byte logical blocks (4Kn) are not supported by the boot path. | A legacy-only device is refused with a message naming it; a 4Kn boot disk is not supported. | Modern virtio (`disable-legacy=on`) and 512-byte logical blocks. | ADR-257; ROADMAP LATER-11 |
| **Two real machines only.** A release-candidate image has run on one netcup aarch64 KVM VPS and one Contabo x86_64 KVM VPS. The x86_64 code after the H7 commit (disk encryption, boot integrity, everything since) has not run on an x86_64 KVM guest or on hardware. | Other providers are expected to work because of their devices; nobody has checked. | Treat a first boot on a new provider as an experiment; the [boot-failure form](../.github/ISSUE_TEMPLATE/boot-failure.yml) is how to report it. | AUDIT.md section 6; ROADMAP NOW-12 |
| **A VPS console that goes dark after the boot menu** ("Display output is not active", netcup ARM). | The boot log is invisible there. | `console.virtio_gpu = "on"` or `"auto"`; or read `$SYS/console/out` over MQTT ([FIRST-HOUR.md](FIRST-HOUR.md) step 4). With `"auto"`, a VM that has both QEMU's `ramfb` and a virtio-gpu gets the virtio-gpu and the `ramfb` picture freezes. | ADR-256; AUDIT A256-W2 |
| **Secure Boot is not used by release images**, and where you sign images yourself: a signed image cannot be configured afterwards (configure, then `sign-iso`), the BIOS path has no signature, Proxmox, physical PCs and cloud firmware are untested, shim/MOK is not supported, and an older signed image boots unless you revoke it in `dbx` (no version counter). | Boot integrity rests on the hypervisor and on the loader's enrolled hashes; Secure Boot rests on the operator's own keys. | Configure first, then sign ([DEPLOY.md](DEPLOY.md) section 5d, [secure-boot-keys.md](secure-boot-keys.md)); on a VPS the firmware is the provider's. | AUDIT H8-W2, H8-W3, H8-W4, H8-W6; ADR-320, ADR-322, ADR-324; ROADMAP LATER-6 |
| **The NIC's receive interrupt (ADR-270) has run under QEMU only** (HVF with a GICv2m frame, TCG with the GICv3 ITS, x86_64 TCG). On aarch64 the ITS device ID is assumed to be the PCI requester ID, as on QEMU/KVM `virt`. | On a hypervisor that delivers the interrupt differently or maps device IDs otherwise, the broker notices after 8 frames that waited and polls every 200 us from then on (a warning says so); a hypervisor that delivers it late adds that delay to idle receive latency. | `[devices] virtio_net_interrupt = "off"` (configuration format 11) forces the polling of v0.4.2. | ADR-270; ROADMAP NOW-13 |
| **virtio-net multiqueue (NEXT-6) has run under QEMU TCG only**, on a multi-queue tap inside Docker on a Mac, with QEMU's software RSS. Multiqueue without RSS (the usual KVM setup) is not used. On aarch64 with only a GICv3 ITS the worker cores' queues are polled. | On a host whose RSS behaves differently the frames of a flow that land on the wrong queue are passed through core 0 (counted in `$SYS/broker/net/queue/forwarded/<k>`); the throughput gain on a real hypervisor is unmeasured. | `[devices] virtio_net_queues = "single"` (configuration format 19) uses one queue pair, as v0.5.0 did. | ADR-310; ADR-314; ROADMAP NEXT-6 |
| **DMA isolation is off by default, and the PCI and virtio parsers are not fuzzed.** | With `devices.iommu = "off"` (the default) a hostile or buggy virtual device is trusted; in a VM the hypervisor is trusted anyway. The IOMMU support (VT-d, AMD-Vi, SMMUv3) ran only against QEMU's models. | Run it under a hypervisor you trust, or set `devices.iommu = "require"` where the host exposes an IOMMU. | AUDIT H0-W9; ADR-305 to ADR-307; ROADMAP NOW-8 |
| **A/B updates (v0.6.0) are offline, on new installs only, and cannot catch a kernel that dies before its block driver runs.** `urmqtt-disk slots` needs the VM stopped; a disk prepared before v0.6.0 stays single-slot (`update-boot`); a kernel that dies before the block driver is not counted (A295-W1) and boots again until `slots rollback` offline; there is no version counter (a rule stops an operator's mistake, not an attacker who can write the disk); no `$SYS` topics for the slot state; the power-cut points are tested under UEFI only; the broker was never updated this way on a VPS. | A kernel that fails after the store opens is rolled back by itself; one that fails earlier is not. | Keep the previous image and test an update on a spare disk first ([DEPLOY.md](DEPLOY.md) 8b, [OPERATE.md](OPERATE.md) "Boot slots and updates"). | ADR-295 to ADR-299; ROADMAP NEXT-11 |

## 5. Storage

| Issue | What it means for you | Workaround | Tracking |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The live entry keeps everything in RAM.** | Retained messages, sessions, telemetry and run-time revocations are gone at the next reboot. | Install to a disk; `storage.mode = "disk"` forbids the live entry. | [DEPLOY.md](DEPLOY.md), "Live mode" |
| **Rolling the data disk back** to an old snapshot restores old revocations. Encryption at rest (XTS) gives no integrity. | A revoked device can come back with an old snapshot. | Also list revocations in the image (`tls.revocation_list`): they are re-applied at every boot. | AUDIT H1-W2; ADR-240 |
| **A crash of the broker while a client is connected loses that session's in-flight state since its last disconnect.** It is written to the disk when the session goes offline. QoS 2 (ADR-271) is exactly once across client disconnects, takeovers and a restart while the client is away, not across a crash while it is connected. | After such a crash a QoS 2 message whose PUBREC the client never saw can be delivered twice, and QoS 1 or 2 messages queued for that session since it connected are lost. | Treat a broker crash as "at least once" for the messages in flight at that moment; make receivers idempotent where a duplicate matters. | AUDIT A271-W1; ADR-271 |
| **A disk passphrase is guessed offline at one PBKDF2 per guess.** The superblock of a copied disk holds the wrapped key; each guess costs PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA-256 at the slot's iterations (300 000 by default, 10 000 accepted), not memory-hard. The passphrase rule is 12 characters. | A short or common passphrase protects little against whoever copies the disk. A passphrase change does not help against an earlier copy of the header (the disk key stays the same). | Use a generated passphrase of 20 characters or more (`openssl rand -base64 24`) and the default cost; `urmqtt-disk keys` warns otherwise. After a leak, `urmqtt-disk rekey`, then `keys add`. | ADR-372; ADR-374 |
| **SQLite reads the data disk without fuzzing of crafted database files.** It runs as an unprivileged, restartable worker. | A crafted disk could make the store give wrong answers or restart. | Treat the data disk as trusted; do not attach disks of unknown origin. | AUDIT H0-W8; ROADMAP NOW-10 |

## 6. Entropy

| Issue | What it means for you | Workaround | Tracking |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A VPS without virtio-rng and without `RNDR`/`RDRAND`** (the netcup ARM VPS is one) seeds the random generator from CPU timing jitter plus the bootloader's EFI RNG: the weakest seeding the kernel accepts. The boot log says `WARN entropy: no hardware source ... CPU jitter fallback`; from v0.4.2 on `$SYS/broker/entropy/source` says `jitter+firmware`, and the builder and `urmqtt-config` warn while the fallback is allowed (ADR-263). | The TLS keys of that machine depend on the jitter estimate, which was measured under QEMU and on that one VPS. | Ask the provider for a virtio-rng; or set `entropy.fallback = "none"` and the image refuses to boot without a hardware source. | ADR-038; ROADMAP NOW-4, D-3 |

## 7. Not proven yet (the "Limits" of v0.4.0 to v0.6.0)

- **AArch64 X25519 assembly** (new in v0.4.1, written for this project): tested against RFC 7748 and another
  implementation; timing records from Docker on an Apple M4 Pro only; no independent review. PSTATE.DIT is never
  set (AUDIT A255-W2). Constant-time evidence does not cover the X25519 key share or Ed25519 (AUDIT.md section 6;
  ROADMAP NOW-9).
- **x86_64:** built and checked by clippy, but its unit tests run nowhere (CI's host tests run on arm64), the
  x86_64 and universal-ISO smoke suites are not in CI, and nothing of v0.4.0 to v0.6.0 ran on an x86_64 KVM guest, apart from v0.5.0 on the Contabo VPS.
- **The Neoverse N1 figures** are from one netcup VPS, one run each, on shared hosts.
- **virtio-console, -balloon, -input and -vsock** have not run on a real VPS; vsock only against a test double.
- **Nothing of v0.6.0 has run on a VPS, on KVM or on physical hardware** (IPv6, Prometheus metrics, A/B updates, run-time
  accounts, the per-address limit, multiqueue and RSS, log shipping over TLS, the IOMMU, sealed Secure Boot images, the
  console disk passphrase): QEMU (HVF and TCG) and the local rig only. Contabo has run v0.5.0. The two-VPS comparison
  of [PERFORMANCE.md](PERFORMANCE.md) section 13 measured v0.4.2 and the SACK build; section 14 is the multiqueue A/B on the
  local TCG rig and claims no throughput gain. The builder test "the build ends in a result card" needs the emulator
  runtime (`make builder-emulator`).
- **Nothing of v0.5.0 ran on a VPS before its release** (QoS 2, MQTT 5.0, WebSocket, publisher back-pressure, the
  receive interrupt, the ADR-268 TCP fix) apart from the production run on Contabo after it.
- **100k and 1M connections** were not generated; the per-connection memory was measured at 10k (AUDIT.md
  section 6).

## Reporting

A limit that is not here, or an item here that is wrong: open an issue (the forms are in
`.github/ISSUE_TEMPLATE/`). A vulnerability: privately, as [SECURITY.md](../SECURITY.md) says.
